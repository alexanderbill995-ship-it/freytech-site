**H4 (link):** Have You Checked Your Alkalinity Today?
> ALKALINITY EXPLAINED, AS IT PERTAINS TO SWIMMING POOLS
> Your Total Alkalinity (TA) is a measure of the alkaline substances that are in the water. In swimming pool water, we are concerned with Bicarbonate Alkalinity. This should be maintained between 80 ppm and 120 ppm.
> When your Total Alkalinity is within this range, it prevents rapid pH changes and stabilizes the pH level. This is also referred to as the pH Buffer.
> If your TA becomes too low, Marcite plaster or Gunite surfaces can become etched, the pool\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\'s walls and floor can stain, and metals may corrode. Low TA levels will cause the pH to bounce around, rapidly going up and down, which will seriously interfere with the normal function of any ORP Controller. It is very common for a pool operator to find the chlorine levels to be extremely high in the mornings due to a very low TA level.
> If your TA becomes too high, it becomes difficult to lower the pH level. Pool water may become cloudy due to high pH levels. Your ORP Controller will be constantly adding acid or CO2 with little to no results. This will also cause your chlorine to lose its effectiveness as a disinfectant. Did you know that CO2 adds alkalinity to your water?
> Frey Technologies strongly recommends that you test your Total Alkalinity regularly. Keeping your TA within range is key to keeping your pool water chemically well balanced.

### ADA Deadline for Pool Lifts Now In Effect (archived, 2012)
- **URL:** https://freytech.org/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=11
- **H1:** ADA Deadline for Pool Lifts Now In Effect
> The requirements outlined in the ADA for pool lifts are found in section 1009. All Aqua Creek Lifts meet these requirements as proven by our independent ADA verification with Wyle Labs. The biggest issue has been the recent Department of Justice "clarifications". We would like to simplify things and express what the DOJ has clarified in simple terms.
>
> The Department of Justice clarified a few basic issues that will affect the purchase of a pool lift as follows:
> - Each body of water needs to have its own form of access.
> - Each form of access needs to be at the poolside and ready to use during operating hours.
> - If a Portable lift is used, it must be "fixed" to the pool deck or apron during operating hours.
> - To fix a lift, it must require tools to remove it.
> - The use of a non-fixed portable lift is acceptable when installation of a fixed unit is not readily achievable.
>
> FreyTech believes in supplying products to our customers that meet the laws to the greatest extent possible. We have several lift models and new additions to meet all the new requirements as outlined above.
>
> For more information:
> http://www.ada.gov/pools_2010.htm
> http://www.arda.org/arda/news-information/default.aspx?id=3583
> http://www.aquacreek.com/adaresources.html
