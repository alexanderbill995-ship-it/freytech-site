## DPD vs FAS-DPD — per Taylor
Blog "How FAS-DPD Tests for Free and Combined Chlorine Values" (May 8, 2025): https://taylortechnologies.com/blogs/news/how-fas-dpd-tests-free-and-combined-chlorine-values
- Standard DPD: "the color of the treated sample is then compared to a set of color standards supplied by the test kit manufacturer."
- FAS-DPD: DPD powder turns the sample pink; "FAS is then added drop by drop until the pink color completely and permanently disappears, signaling the endpoint of the reaction." "The number of drops used to cause this color change is multiplied by the appropriate factor for the size of the water sample."
- Range: measures chlorine "as low as 0.2 ppm—the maximum allowable level for combined chlorine according to the Pool & Hot Tub Alliance (PHTA)—and as high as 20 ppm."
- Why operators prefer it: eliminates "the need for color matching", avoids the case where "any color has been bleached out of the sample" at high sanitizer levels, and "makes it easier to use for those with color vision deficiencies." "Most service professionals and health officials prefer testing chlorine with FAS-DPD."
- FAQ on bleaching: "High sanitizer levels, specifically greater than 10 ppm, can cause total or partial bleaching, leading to false-low readings" — dilute and retest.

## Reagent shelf life & storage — per Taylor
Blog "Pool Testing Reagents: Care, Storage and Shelf Life" (Feb 11, 2025): https://taylortechnologies.com/blogs/news/pool-testing-reagents-care-storage-and-shelf-life
- "formulated to remain effective for at least one year"; recommendation to replace reagents at the beginning of each pool season; R-0718 Silver Nitrate should be "replaced on an annual basis".
- "Store reagents at a consistent temperature in the range of 36°– 85°F (2°–29°C)"; avoid prolonged direct sunlight; store away from treatment chemicals; recap immediately; never swap caps or decant; thaw frozen reagents at room temperature only.
- Degradation signs: DPD #2 darkens brown/pink, DPD #3 turns yellow, Phenol Red shifts yellow/purple; undissolved floating particles.
- FAQ: "All reagents and test strips have a best by date and lot number printed on the label." "It is recommended to replace your reagents and test strips seasonally." (Taylor does not publish a "2-year" figure on these pages — do not state one.)
- Companion blog: https://taylortechnologies.com/blogs/news/caring-for-pool-and-spa-test-kits-and-reagents
